INTERACTIVE STORY OF A STUDENT ROCKOON

Three students at CHIREC International School, Hyderabad, set out to fly the highest student-led rocket in the world. Not from a launch pad, but from 30 km up, hanging beneath a hydrogen balloon.

A 2022–2023 proposal that never left paper. This is the flight it was designed to make.

BALLOON-ASSISTED ASCENT

Strap the rocket beneath a high-altitude hydrogen balloon and let buoyancy carry it the first 30 km, above the densest air, then ignite. The proposal estimated the balloon would cut the cost of the project by nearly 25–30%.

THE ROCKET

REUSABLE HYPERGOLIC SOUNDING ROCKET

A 301 stainless-steel airframe coated in polyurethane against heating near the ozone layer and across the Kármán line. At the top, a 5–6 kg chamber for equipment the school wanted to send to space, with cameras at three different angles. Every component was meant to be tracked, collected, refilled and reused.

THE KÁRMÁN
LINE

100 KM · THE EDGE OF SPACE

Measured from the ground, a 30 km balloon start leaves 70 km for the rocket; measured from release, the goal was 100 km more. Either way: higher than any student-led rocket before it. Then home again: the team planned to predict the landing with thrust-to-weight formulae and GPS, and recover the rocket and parachute for reuse.
